How Parents Can Support Their Child's Learning

Practical guidance for parents supporting children in online, home and school-connected learning.

Find a TutorBecome a Tutor

Parents do not need to become the teacher

A parent's most useful role is often to create the conditions in which learning can happen consistently. This means supporting routines, communication, confidence and a suitable learning environment.

Create a realistic routine

A predictable study time can help children develop habits. The routine should match the child's age, school workload, energy and other responsibilities. More hours do not automatically mean better learning.

Provide a suitable learning environment

For online tuition, check that the child has a workable device, connection and reasonably quiet space. For home tuition, provide a comfortable place where tutor and student can work without unnecessary interruptions.

Focus on progress, not comparison

Children develop at different rates. Ask what the child understands now, what remains difficult and what support would help. Comparing them with siblings, classmates or other children can reduce confidence without solving the learning problem.

Communicate with the tutor

Useful communication is specific: share relevant observations, ask about learning goals and discuss practical concerns. Tutors can provide learning observations, while parents can provide context about routines and the child's broader experience.

Support independent learning

As children grow, gradually shift responsibility toward them. Encourage them to organise materials, attempt problems, ask questions and reflect on mistakes rather than depending on an adult for every answer.

When additional support may help

If a child repeatedly struggles with a particular concept, routine or learning situation, discuss the pattern with the relevant teacher or tutor. Persistent or significant concerns may require advice from qualified educational or other appropriate professionals.
